# portfolio: Project Notes

## Sources
- Max's public LinkedIn profile: the career facts.
- Max's public GitHub repositories: every project description.
- Max's own travel write-ups: every side-quest number.
- Max's 2022 capstone script for his summer at the Rhode Island refuges: Summer in the Woods.

## Critical assumptions
- Every number on the page traces to one of the sources above. Where two sources disagreed the
  number was left out.
- Only public projects are shown.
- Map interchanges are true by place: Stockholm (semester abroad, European Excursions),
  Washington (a summer at the CFPB, the Smithsonian visits), New York (Brattle, StationScope,
  Hide and Seek; all three are named on the map, Max, 2026-11-22). The map carries no dates.
- The Life Dashboard strip is rendered from that project's sample fixture, not from real data.
- The portrait is Max's GitHub profile picture, copied to `public/max.jpg`.

## Design decisions
- A quiet editorial page set in one family, IBM Plex Sans (Max, 2026-11-20: the serif headings
  read as professional but not as him; he wanted something cleaner, square, and modern, academic
  without being bookish). Headings are weight 500 with slightly tight tracking, entry titles 600,
  and the large numbers weight 300, so the hierarchy holds without a second family.
- One calm dark ground (pine green by default) carries the hero and the footer. A three-line network map (work, things built,
  side quests) is the single themed element, and the quest line continues down the side-quest list.
- Colour: nature tones (Max, 2026-11-17: the indigo and neon set read as a default template).
  Wheat, terracotta, and lake blue for the three lines on pine green; cream paper and bark ink.
  All text pairs measure 4.6:1 or better. The links in the work paragraph and in the side-quest text
  take their line's colour and keep their underline (Max, 2026-11-22). For that the default wheat on paper is the
  wheat ink #83692c (4.6:1 on cream), the same value the rotating palettes use, replacing
  #c9962b (2.4:1).
- The side-quest line runs on two tracks and changes track between entries, using the hero map's
  45 degree bends with rounded corners. The text keeps one left edge; only the line and stations
  move. The line opens with a lead-in bend and closes with a terminus bar. The lead-in starts level
  with the section heading and never rises above the section (Max, 2026-11-22), so on wide screens
  the first entry sits one bend below the heading. An entry reached from
  the map (`:target`) fills its station in lake blue.
- Vertical rhythm is tight (Max, 2026-11-19: too much white space). The gap between sections is
  `--space-8`, 40 px on a phone rising to 72 px on a wide screen; spacing inside sections is
  unchanged.
- On wide screens (56rem and up) the side-quest entries sit either side of the line (Max,
  2026-11-19: the column under the heading was empty). Each entry's `side` is set in its file and
  defaults to right; Holland and the Smithsonian are on the left, with right-aligned text, because
  that split leaves the two columns closest to even. Each station is on the track nearest its
  entry. It is built with CSS subgrid; browsers without subgrid, and phones, keep one column.
- "A Summer in the Woods" is the first side quest. It is told as a side quest, not as work
  experience: the house in the woods, the trail, the books, the oatmeal (Max, 2026-11-19). The
  capstone video is unlisted and is not linked.
- The side-quest line starts at Summer in the Woods. The dashed fork to Max's cooking account was
  removed (Max, 2026-11-20: it read as out of place); the page no longer links to Instagram.
- Arriving at an entry from the map (`:target`) plays a short arrival signal: two rings in the
  line colour leave the side-quest station, the card's edge, or the Work heading's station, then
  stop (Max, 2026-11-20). It waits 0.6 s so the smooth scroll lands first, and does not run under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- On the wide map the Summer in the Woods stop is raised and Hamilton College lowered so their
  labels do not crowd each other (Max, 2026-11-20).
- Only cards whose number says something carry one. StationScope is the featured card: a plain card whose outline runs through the three line
  colours (Max, 2026-11-22: the dark ground and glow were an eyesore).
- Career appears as two short paragraphs, not a list. The employer is named once.
- Self-contained: outside links are LinkedIn, GitHub, Max's own apps, the organisations named in
  the work paragraph, and context links (Max, 2026-11-21): a named thing a stranger may not know
  links to its official page, inline on the phrase. Currently the Amtrak rail pass
  (amtrak.com/usa-rail-pass), the Smithsonian museums (si.edu/museums), Jet Lag
  (nebula.tv/jetlag), and the Treasury (home.treasury.gov). Rhode Island's national wildlife refuges
  link to the Wikipedia article on the refuge complex, because the Fish and Wildlife Service has
  a page per refuge but none found for the complex as a whole. amtrak.com and si.edu refuse
  scripted requests with a 403, so those two were confirmed by web search, not by fetching.
- The three line colours change on each page load (Max, 2026-11-20). A short inline script in the
  document head assigns the lines their colours before first paint and never repeats the last
  set shown in that tab (it keeps the set's number in `sessionStorage`; nothing is sent anywhere).
  With scripts off the page shows the default palette.
- The colours are not random. There are 19 tones in `tokens.css`, each with a value for the pine
  ground and a darker ink for the cream page, and 13 sets of three that were measured together:
  apricot lavender mint; butter rose lavender; rose sand periwinkle; wheat lavender mint; wheat
  seafoam iris; terracotta aqua iris; coral ice iris; peach lake iris; marigold mint iris; straw
  rose periwinkle; cream aqua iris; terracotta lavender ice; butter lake iris. The script picks a
  set and deals its three tones to the lines in any order, so with the default there are 79
  combinations. Measured: every tone is 4.9:1 or better on the pine ground and 6.8:1 or better
  on the dark page, every ink is 4.6:1 or better on cream, and within each set the three colours
  stay apart (CIELAB distance 22 or more) under simulated deuteranopia, protanopia, and
  tritanopia. Most sets carry a purple because that is what stays distinct from a warm tone and a
  blue-green under red-green colour blindness. A new set must pass the same checks.
- The ground colour changes on each page load too (Max, 2026-11-20: calm colours only, matched
  to the line colours). The same script picks one of six grounds, never the one shown last, then
  a tone set allowed on it. The grounds are pine #1f3d31 (the default), fjord #1d3a47, dusk
  #2a3750, plum #45313f, bark #453329, and slate #30383f. All five new ones were built at pine's
  luminance, so `--on-sky` text is 10.4:1 or better on each and every tone keeps the contrast it
  has on pine (lowest 4.9:1). Each ground has its own muted text at 7:1. The page colours below
  the hero stay fixed in both themes.
- Pairing rule: a tone within 30 degrees of the ground's hue (OKLCH) is allowed only if it is
  7.4:1 or better on it, the margin mint already has on pine, and a set may hold one such tone.
  Slate is exempt as near neutral. Allowed sets: pine and slate take all 14; fjord takes all but
  the default and the two lake sets; dusk takes the default, apricot lavender mint, butter rose
  lavender, wheat lavender mint, and terracotta lavender ice; plum takes all but the three rose
  sets; bark takes butter rose lavender, rose sand periwinkle, wheat lavender mint, wheat seafoam
  iris, peach lake iris, straw rose periwinkle, cream aqua iris, and butter lake iris. That is 63
  ground and set pairs and 358 combinations counting line order. Wine and moss grounds were
  tried and rejected as too loud and too close to pine. The browser bar colour follows the
  ground. The favicon stays pine and wheat.
- No other client JavaScript. All motion is CSS and stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The site is served from `/portfolio` on GitHub Pages; internal URLs use `BASE_URL`.
